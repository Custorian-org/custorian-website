1. Our approach to good governance

Custorian is a Danish frivillig forening, a voluntary non-profit membership association. It is not an erhvervsdrivende fond, so the Danish Anbefalinger for god Fondsledelse do not apply to us as a matter of law.

We measure ourselves against them anyway, and publish the result, including where we fall short. We ask platforms to prove their claims about children rather than assert them, and a body that asks for proof has to show its own. Nothing below is marked as complied with because we intend to do it.

3. Governing principles

Five principles decide the hard cases. Each has a corresponding structural rule, so that following the principle is not a matter of goodwill.

  1. Independence before growth
    Where a funding route, partnership or member would give an assessable platform influence over the standard, we decline it, even when we need the money. Rule: protected provisions in the articles, a 30 per cent single-source funding cap, and an outright bar on funding from any platform that is or could become assessable.
  2. Evidence, not assertion
    Every control in the standard is written to be evidenced by a third party. We hold our own claims to the same bar, which is why this page states what is not yet in place as plainly as what is. Rule: decisions are published with their rationale, and claims of partnership or endorsement are published only when current and verifiable.
  3. Children are the subject, not the data source
    Safety work done to children rather than for them fails on its own terms. Rule: a standing youth seat on the Advisory Board, safeguarding sign-off before any activity involving children, and a research arm with no monetary tie to any platform it studies.
  4. We maintain the standard, we do not grade against it
    The body that writes a test should not mark the papers. Rule: platform certification is issued only by bodies accredited to ISO/IEC 17065, and the association never audits or certifies a platform.
  5. Open by default, closed only for a stated reason
    Documents, decisions and accounts are public unless publishing them would expose a child, a named individual, an unfinished deliberation or sensitive investigative material. Where we withhold, we say that we are withholding, and why.

How seats are filled

Seats are filled by resolution of the sitting board and presented to the next general assembly for ratification. The general assembly may ratify or refuse an appointment but may not itself appoint; where an appointment is refused, the board appoints again. Appointment requires a two-thirds board majority. Commercial platforms and services that are or may become subject to assessment under the standard, and companies controlling or controlled by them, are not eligible for membership and hold no seat.

Independence

At least two directors must be independent. A director is independent if, within the preceding three years, they have not been employed by, received fees from, or held ownership or a management position in the association, an entity it controls, a certification body under the scheme, or a platform certified or seeking certification, and are not a close relative of, or in a close personal relationship with, anyone who has. Independence is confirmed in writing on appointment and annually. If the number of independent directors falls below two, the board must fill the vacancy within six months.

Term

Two years, renewable. Each seat above carries its term expiry date once filled.

The chair and executive arrangement, stated plainly

The founder holds a seat by right, chairs the board until an independent chair is elected, and serves as interim managing director. The board is required to seek an independent chair by 31 December 2028, and the interim executive appointment ends no later than 31 December 2029 unless extended by a majority including every independent director. Both are time-boxed continuity arrangements in a young association, and both are departures from what good practice would normally prefer, which is why they are published here rather than left in the articles for someone to find. The independent vice chair is the counterweight, and that seat is open.

Remuneration

Board service is unpaid. Documented, reasonable expenses may be reimbursed and are disclosed in the annual accounts.

Protected provisions

No majority, including a unanimous one, can change the association's purpose, sell or exclusively licence the standard, or admit as members platforms subject to assessment. The technical content of the standard sits outside the general assembly's vote entirely and is set through the technical governance process.

6. Advisory Board

A roster of ten to twelve named individual experts across child rights, legal and regulatory, technology, academic, clinical, law enforcement and youth perspectives. Members give ad hoc, non-binding input on the standard, its ethical dimensions and its child-facing content. They hold no vote and cannot bind the board; where the board declines material advice, it records that it did so. Members serve in a personal expert capacity: an affiliation is listed for identification only and is not a partnership, endorsement or review by their employer.

Three sub-committees

The Standards and Technical Sub-Committee carries the quarterly update cycle for the standard and invites stakeholder institutions as consultees. The Certification and Impartiality Sub-Committee protects the certification firewall, recognises certification bodies, arbitrates the final scheme instance of the appeals ladder, and runs an annual impartiality review; it carries between three and five members, excludes anyone from the certification or commercial arms, excludes the founder from membership and from chairing, and elects its own chair. The Policy and Rights Sub-Committee grounds the work in regulatory, academic and societal developments.

Advisory seats are unpaid and non-voting, and are formalised individually by memorandum of understanding. A youth advisory member may receive an honorarium. Where a youth member is under eighteen, guardian consent is obtained, engagement is age-appropriate, the member never accesses case or offender-related material in any form, and the safeguarding lead signs off the engagement design in advance.

7. Independence and conflicts

Everyone who takes a decision for Custorian, whether director, advisory member, ethics reviewer, officer or volunteer, declares any personal, professional or financial interest that could reasonably be seen to affect that decision, and declares it before the decision is taken rather than after.

What must be declared

Employment, consultancy, board seats, shareholdings, grants and close personal relationships involving: any platform that is or could become assessable under the standard, any certification body under the scheme, any applicant for funding, any research partner, the commercial arm, and any intelligence provider, registrar or hosting provider that could appear in our published infrastructure work.

What happens then

The interest is entered in the register. The person withdraws from both the discussion and the vote. Where recusal would leave the board without a quorum of unconflicted members, the decision is deferred, not taken by a compromised board, and deferral does not count as approval. A quorum shortage never overrides a recusal.

Paid engagement of a director or advisor

Where the association engages a director's or advisor's professional services separately from their unpaid governance role, the engagement is disclosed, the individual is recused from approving their own engagement, the terms are benchmarked at arm's-length market rates, the total across all such engagements is capped as a share of annual expenditure with any exception recorded and mitigated, and the engagement is entered in the register.

9. How the work is kept apart

Five arms

Operations sit in five arms, each with its own scope and funding source, so no single arm can quietly capture the others. An Association arm handles membership, fundraising and donor stewardship, with a strict firewall against platform funding. A Standards arm owns the standard and control framework. A Certification arm manages the scheme and professional credentialing, and never audits a platform. A Research arm runs studies and corpus validation, funded solely by grants. A commercial Tech arm builds and sells technology under the standard, licensing intellectual property from the association at arm's length; no raw or lightly processed personal data crosses that licence, and the licence reverts automatically on insolvency, hostile takeover or material ethical breach.

The association does not certify platforms

Platform certification is issued exclusively by independent bodies accredited to ISO/IEC 17065 by a national accreditation body under Regulation (EC) 765/2008. The association maintains the scheme and licenses the mark to qualifying bodies, and never audits or certifies a platform itself. A separate in-house track credentials professionals, auditors and child safety officers, under an ISO/IEC 17024 approach. Credentialing people is not the same activity as certifying platforms, and neither is confused with the other. If the association ever establishes its own certification body, no director may sit on its board, no staff may be shared, its revenue may not be tied to certification volume or outcome, and a twenty-four month cooling-off period applies in both directions.

Eleven firewalls

A standard is only independent if it cannot be captured. These are the rules that make capture structurally difficult rather than merely discouraged.

  1. Certification independence. Only external accredited bodies decide certification, and no one from the commercial arm sits on the committee that protects that firewall.
  2. Advice is not audit. The association and the commercial arm may both advise, but neither ever audits, and their records stay separated.
  3. Research impartiality. No monetary tie between the research arm and any platform under study.
  4. Data firewall. Research data stays separate from commercial product telemetry, and no raw data crosses the licence to the commercial arm.
  5. Anti-capture. No corporate platform donors, and arm's-length pricing on anything commercial.
  6. Founder conflicts. The founder recuses from any decision involving a declared conflict.
  7. Mission lock. The association holds no outside shares, and the intellectual property licence terminates on breach and reverts automatically on insolvency, hostile takeover or material ethical breach.
  8. Data protection independence. The data protection lead reports directly to the board, sits outside the revenue-generating arms, and is never the chair or managing director.
  9. Committee overlap. No certification or commercial arm staff on the impartiality committee, the founder may not sit on or chair it, and no director directs its substantive decisions.
  10. No capture of certification bodies. Professional credentialing is preferred for certification bodies, never a mandatory gate.
  11. No platform equity in the commercial arm. No assessable platform, and no entity in which one holds a material interest, may hold equity in or control the commercial arm.
Why this page exists. Custorian asks platforms to prove their claims about children rather than assert them. We hold ourselves to the same test. Governance you cannot inspect is a marketing claim; governance you can inspect is a commitment.

10. Raising a concern

Anyone, inside or outside Custorian, can raise a serious concern about how the association is run.

What belongs here

Fraud, theft or misuse of funds; bribery or corruption; a conflict of interest concealed rather than declared; a breach of the firewalls above, for example a platform obtaining influence over the standard or a certification decision; a safeguarding failure; a serious breach of data protection; or retaliation against someone who raised any of these.

What belongs elsewhere

Disagreeing with a decision, appealing a rejected membership or funding application, or disputing a certification outcome. A rejected application is not a governance failure. Certification disputes follow the published appeals ladder, which begins with the certification body that issued the decision.

How to raise one, and what protection applies

Write to info@custorian.org. Where the concern involves the chair, it is directed to the vice chair once that seat is filled, and meanwhile to an advisory board member not otherwise involved, so that a concern about a person is never decided by that person. You are protected against retaliation whether or not the concern turns out to be founded, provided you raised it in good faith. We acknowledge within five working days and make an initial assessment within fifteen.

A dedicated independent reporting channel, monitored outside the executive line, is being established alongside the appointment of the independent directors. Until it exists, the route above is the route, and we say so rather than describing a channel that is not yet running.

13. Decisions and minutes

The board publishes a summary of every material decision with the reasoning behind it. We publish approved decisions and their rationale rather than verbatim minutes, which keeps individuals, third parties and unfinished deliberations out of the public record while keeping the association's choices fully accountable. The first entries appear once the inaugural board is seated.

14. Finances

The financial year follows the calendar year, and the first ran from founding to 31 December 2026. Accounts are kept by the chair, reviewed and signed off by a lay auditor who is not a board member, recommended to the general assembly by a two-thirds board majority, and are not final until the general assembly approves them. A registered or state-authorised auditor is elected once the association receives material project funding or applies for public-benefit status.

No single funder may exceed roughly 30 per cent of annual income, and the association maintains a reserve target of three to six months of operating expenses. Separately from the concentration limit, the association accepts no funding at all from any platform that is or could become assessable under the standard, or from any entity controlled or substantially funded by one. Funds found to have been received contrary to that bar are returned or redirected to a charitable purpose unconnected with the donor.

15. Membership and support

Foreningen Custorian has a single class of membership, Support Membership, open to youth representatives, parents, trust and safety experts, non-profit civil-society organisations, and for-profit organisations that are not assessable under the standard. Platforms and services that are or may become subject to assessment, and companies controlling or controlled by them, are not eligible: a platform that could be assessed under the standard cannot also sit inside the body that owns it.

Support Members do not appoint directors. Board seats are filled by board resolution and put to the general assembly for ratification, and the general assembly may ratify or refuse an appointment but may not itself appoint. Support Members attend the general assembly, propose agenda items, and vote on every other matter reserved to it, including the accounts. Voting requires three months' unbroken membership and any fee due to be paid.

16. Donations

Custorian is permitted by Indsamlingsnævnet, the Danish Fundraising Board, to collect public donations from 12 August 2026 to 11 August 2027, for project expenses that advance children's safety online and for operating costs. This channel is for individual donations only. Custorian accepts no donation, grant or sponsorship from any platform, company or organisation assessable under the standard. Giving stays separate from influence, the same firewall that shapes membership.

Recurring donations can be cancelled at any time before the next payment, with no fee, through the Stripe customer portal link in your receipt and renewal emails, or by writing to info@custorian.org, confirmed within two business days. Donations are voluntary gifts, final once received, and are not currently tax-deductible in Denmark.
