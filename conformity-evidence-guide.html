Custorian · Conformity Evidence Guide
Custorian

How to prove it.

A platform does not declare itself safe for children. It produces evidence. This guide sets out, control by control, the evidence a digital service generates to demonstrate conformity with the Custorian standard — 139 auditable controls across six domains.

Foreningen Custorian CVR 46399455 Companion to the Custorian Standard, Draft EN v0.2

How conformity works

Custorian is a conformity standard, not a self-assessment badge. Conformity is determined against documented, measurable controls and evidenced by artefacts an independent assessor can inspect, test, and reproduce.

  • 139 controls, six domains. Each control is a single auditable requirement. 130 are required for conformity; 9 are addressable — applicable where relevant, with a documented justification if not implemented.
  • SHALL vs SHOULD. A SHALL control is mandatory. A SHOULD control is recommended; non-implementation must be justified and recorded.
  • Certified by independent bodies. The foreningen owns the scheme and licenses the “Custorian Certified” mark; certification itself is issued by independent accredited Certification Bodies under EC 765/2008. The foreningen does not certify platforms directly, and no vendor is privileged.
  • Evidence travels. Most artefacts below already exist for platforms meeting DSA, GDPR, the UK AADC, the Online Safety Act or COPPA. Custorian maps them to one coherent child-safety frame, so you produce them once.
What an assessor expects. For every applicable control: a named owner, a current artefact (dated, version-controlled), and — where the control is testable — a reproducible result. “We do this” is not evidence. “Here is the configuration, the test, the log, and who signed it off” is.

Six kinds of evidence

Every control is satisfied by one or more of these artefact types. The tables that follow tag each control with the primary type(s) expected.

P Policy / procedure — a documented, dated, owned written control.
C Configuration / system export — the actual settings, schema, or architecture in effect.
T Test / metrics report — a reproducible measurement, ideally independently produced.
L Logs / records — operational evidence the control runs in production.
A Attestation / contract — a third-party assurance, DPA, or signed clause.
D Demonstration — a screenshot, recorded walkthrough, or published page (URL).

Rows shaded grey are addressable controls (SHOULD): produce the evidence if applicable, or a recorded justification for non-applicability.

From evidence to certification

  • 1 · Assemble the pack. Gather the artefacts above for every applicable control. Most platforms already hold the majority through existing DSA/GDPR/AADC work.
  • 2 · Pre-screen. Custorian runs an automated first-look assessment of your live service to identify obvious gaps before formal review.
  • 3 · Conformity assessment. An independent accredited Certification Body reviews the evidence pack and tests the live service against the controls.
  • 4 · Certified. On conformity, the “Custorian Certified” mark is issued under licence, with annual surveillance and re-assessment.
Founding cohort. Pilot platforms are assessed first and help shape the certification scheme. Apply at custorian.org.
