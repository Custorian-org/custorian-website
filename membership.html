1. Welcome

Foreningen Custorian is a Danish non-profit association that owns and stewards an open standard for children’s digital safety — the Custorian Controls Framework (155 controls across six domains). The standard is free for anyone to implement, published under CC BY-SA 4.0, and structurally protected against capture by any commercial interest.

Members are the association’s foundation. Under Danish foreningsret, members — not funders, not companies, not the board alone — are the ultimate authority: the general assembly (generalforsamling) is the association’s highest body (§4.1).

2. What membership is — and what it is not

Membership means you support an open, independent child-safety standard, you receive regular member updates with inside insight into the association’s progress — new standard versions, pilots, research, and how membership fees were used — you may attend and vote at the general assembly, and you may stand for election to the Board. Membership fees go directly to the mission (§2.2: all income is applied to the association’s purpose).

Membership does not mean — and can never be — any of the following: - It does not confer the “Custorian Certified” mark, endorsement, or any indication that a member’s product is safe for children. Certification is earned only through independent, accredited certification bodies — never through membership. - It does not give any influence over certification decisions, platform grades, or the technical content of the standard beyond the equal, democratic vote every member holds. - It cannot be bought. Every member has exactly one vote (§4.5), regardless of fee paid or size. Fees are nominal and exist to support the mission, not to purchase standing.

This standard cannot be bought — and neither can a seat at its table.

3. Who can be a member

Membership is open to individuals and civil-society / non-profit organisations who support the association’s purpose. Specifically:

Category Who Annual fee (proposed)
Individual Any person supporting the mission 200 DKK
Student / youth Enrolled students; people under 26 50 DKK (GA may set to 0)
Civil society / NGO Non-profit organisations in child rights, safety, digital wellbeing 500 DKK
Honorary Conferred by the Board (e.g. advisory board members, notable contributors) Free

Commercial platforms and companies are not eligible for membership. This is deliberate: a platform that could be assessed against the standard must not also sit inside the body that owns it. Companies engage with Custorian only through the pilot and certification (assessed by independent accredited bodies), or as donors — and a donation is not membership and confers no rights or influence.

Fees are set by the general assembly (Danish norm). The figures above are the Board’s proposal pending ratification. Fees are annual (calendar year).

4. Member rights and responsibilities

Rights (statutes §§3–5): - Attend the annual general assembly, held once per year by 30 June (§4.2) - One vote on all decisions, taken by simple majority (§4.5) (voting requires three months’ membership before the assembly — anti-capture safeguard, §4.8; members admitted at founding exempt) - Propose items for the general assembly agenda (§4.4) - Stand for election to the Board of Directors (bestyrelse), 1–5 members, two-year terms (§5.1) - Request an extraordinary general assembly (with at least one-third of members) (§4.7) - Receive member updates and take part in public consultations on the standard

Responsibilities: - Act in accordance with the association’s purpose and interests (a member acting against them may be excluded — §3.3) - Pay the annual fee (kontingent) where applicable - Disclose any conflict of interest — in particular, any employment by or representation of a platform that is, or may become, subject to assessment. Such a disclosure does not affect any assessment; non-disclosure is a breach of good standing.

5. How to join

  1. Submit a membership enquiry (custorian.org → Become a member, or email info@custorian.org).
  2. The Board admits members by decision (§3.1) — a light check that the applicant supports the purpose and is eligible (individual or civil-society, not a commercial platform).
  3. On admission, pay the annual fee. Membership runs for the calendar year.

6. Resignation and exclusion

  • Resignation: membership is voluntary; a member may resign at any time by written notice to the Board (§3.2). Fees already paid are not refunded.
  • Exclusion: the Board may exclude a member who acts against the association’s purpose or interests; the excluded member may appeal to the general assembly (§3.3).

7. Governance in brief

  • General assembly — highest authority; elects the Board; approves accounts and fees (§4).
  • Board of Directors — 1–5 members, elected for two-year terms; elects a chair (who also serves as treasurer unless decided otherwise) (§5). Board service is voluntary (unpaid or token honoraria only). Terms are staggered so no single assembly can replace the whole board (§5.1).
  • Advisory Board — 7–9 experts across child rights, law & regulation, technology & security, academic research, and law enforcement, plus a youth representative (16–21). It advises; it does not govern.
  • Certification — issued by independent accredited certification bodies (§5.6); neither the general assembly, the board, nor members may influence individual certification decisions.

Governance safeguards (statutes v1.2, adopted 21 July 2026)

The statutes entrench the association’s independence. In summary:

  • Founder anchoring, time-boxed (§5.2). The founder holds a board seat by right and chairs the board until 31 December 2031; thereafter the chair is elected like any other. This secures continuity through the build phase without permanent personal control. The chair’s tie-breaking vote (§5.4) is unchanged and standard.
  • Mission lock (§10). While the founder is a member, no majority — however large — can change the association’s purpose, sell or exclusively license the standard, the name, or the trademarks, admit commercial companies as members, or amend these protections, without her written consent. This protects the mission, not day-to-day decisions: the general assembly still elects the board, approves accounts, and can outvote the founder on ordinary business.
  • Technical carve-out (§5.6). The technical content of the standard (controls, scoring, methodology) and individual certification decisions are outside the general assembly’s competence — they follow the technical governance process and independent accredited certification bodies. Members can replace the people; no membership majority can vote a control out of the standard or vote a platform a certificate.
  • Anti-capture safeguards (§§3.1, 4.8, 5.1). Three months’ membership before voting rights; staggered board terms; the “no commercial members” rule written into the statutes themselves.

8. Fees and payment

  • Fees are annual and set by the general assembly.
  • Payment by bank transfer or MobilePay (details on invoice / on the account once opened).
  • Note on donations and tax: membership fees fund the association’s operations. Voluntary donations are separate and warmly welcomed; only donations (not membership fees) may in future count toward a §8A tax-deduction scheme, should the association pursue one. See the fundraising brief.

9. Member data and privacy

The association holds only the member data necessary to administer membership (name, contact, category, fee status), processed under GDPR, never sold or shared, and deleted on resignation subject to bookkeeping-law retention. Conflict-of-interest disclosures relating to Board/Advisory roles may be published per the governance firewalls.
