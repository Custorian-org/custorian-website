How Custorian is governed

The board

Foreningen Custorian is governed by a Board of Directors elected by the general assembly. Under the articles (§ 5, v1.2), the board serves staggered two-year terms and includes a Chair, Vice Chair, Treasurer, Secretary and one or more Non-Executive Directors. The founder holds a seat by right and chairs the board until 31 December 2031 — a time-boxed continuity arrangement, published openly here; thereafter the board elects its chair. Protected provisions (§ 10) prevent any majority from changing the association’s purpose, selling or exclusively licensing the standard, or admitting commercial members, and the standard’s technical content sits outside the general assembly’s vote (§ 5.6). Commercial companies — including any platform that is or may become subject to assessment under the standard — are not eligible for membership (§ 3.1) and hold no board seats. The board oversees five operating arms (below) but recuses from the day-to-day decisions of the advisory bodies and the Certification & Impartiality Committee.

Advisory bodies

Two separate, non-governing bodies feed independent perspective into the standard, neither holds a vote and neither can bind the board. An Advisory Board of 7–10 named individual experts, spanning child rights, legal and regulatory, technology, academic and clinical practice, law enforcement, and youth perspectives, gives ad hoc, non-binding input on the standard, its ethical dimensions, and child-facing content. A separate Standards Advisory Committee of up to seven industry practitioners and organisations reviews the technical evolution and real-world implementability of the standard. The two bodies are deliberately kept apart, no shared seats, no overlap in remit.

Five arms, kept apart on purpose

Operations sit in five arms, each with its own scope and funding source, so that no single arm can quietly capture the others. An Association arm handles membership, fundraising and donor stewardship, with a strict firewall against platform funding. A Standards arm owns the standard and control framework, independent of any commercial funding. A Certification arm manages the certification scheme and professional credentialing, and never audits a platform directly. A Research arm runs studies and corpus validation, funded solely by grants. And a commercial Tech arm builds and sells technology under the standard, licensing research and IP from the association at arm’s length — the standard and certification scheme stay independent of it regardless.

The association does not certify platforms

Platform certification is issued exclusively by independent bodies accredited under ISO/IEC 17065, operating under a published scheme; the association maintains the scheme and licenses the “Custorian Certified” trademark to qualifying bodies, and never audits or certifies a platform itself. A separate, in-house professional credentialing track (for auditors and child safety officers) runs under ISO/IEC 17024 through the Certification arm — credentialing people is not the same activity as certifying platforms, and neither is confused with the other.

The firewalls

A standard is only independent if it cannot be captured. Ten firewalls keep it that way: only external, accredited bodies decide platform certification, and no one from the Tech arm sits on that oversight; the association and the Tech arm may both advise, but neither ever audits, and their records stay separated; the Research arm holds no monetary ties to any platform it studies, and research data is kept apart from commercial product data; the association takes no corporate donors and prices any commercial work at arm’s length; the founder recuses from any decision touching their own employer; the association itself has no shares to sell, and any IP licence to the Tech arm terminates on breach; the Data Protection Officer reports independently, outside the revenue-generating arms; no one from the Certification arm or the Tech arm sits on the Impartiality Committee; and professional credentialing is preferred for certification bodies, never a mandatory gate. Every board decision is published with its rationale. There is no pay-to-play.

Why this page exists. Custorian asks platforms to prove their claims about children rather than assert them. We hold ourselves to the same test. Governance you cannot inspect is a marketing claim; governance you can inspect is a commitment.

Decisions & minutes

The board publishes a summary of every material decision, with the reasoning behind it. We publish approved decisions and their rationale rather than verbatim minutes — this keeps individuals, third parties and unfinished deliberations out of the public record while keeping the association’s choices fully accountable.
