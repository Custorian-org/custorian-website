Why this exists

Why now.

On 3 April 2026, the EU lost its legal basis for voluntary child-safety detection on private communications. The temporary derogation to the ePrivacy Directive expired. The proposed Child Sexual Abuse Regulation remains in trilogue with no agreed text. Platforms operating in the EU face the broadest compliance vacuum in the modern history of EU technology regulation, alongside obligations under the DSA, AI Act, and member-state implementations.

The next regulation will not arrive in a vacuum. It will arrive into a market that has had eighteen months without a clear standard. Custorian is the standard built for that arrival.

The standard

The standard.

The Custorian Controls Framework is the first open European compliance standard for child digital safety. 137 auditable controls across 6 domains. Cross-mapped to 10 regulatory frameworks including the DSA, AI Act, AADC, OSA, COPPA, GDPR, and the UNCRC.

The framework is published on Zenodo under Creative Commons. It is freely usable by anyone — platforms, regulators, auditors, academics, civil society, vendors. Adoption requires no licence, no fee, and no agreement with the Foundation.

This standard cannot be bought.

The Custorian Controls Framework is owned by Foreningen Custorian, a Danish non-profit association (CVR 46399455). It cannot be acquired, controlled, or licensed exclusively by any platform it covers. The Foundation cannot be sold. The standard cannot be taken private. The certification programme cannot be captured by a single vendor.

This is structural, not aspirational. The framework is published on Zenodo under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0. This licence cannot be revoked. The Foundation's statutes and the governance separation between the Foundation and any commercial implementer are designed so the standard remains independent permanently.

How it works

How the standard works.

The Custorian Standard consists of three components: the Controls Framework defining what platforms serving children must do, the API Specification defining how compliant systems implement detection and reporting, and the Certification Programme through which platforms are independently validated.

Compliance is assessed through Qualified Safety Assessor audits for the largest platforms, Self-Assessment Questionnaires for smaller platforms, and accuracy metrics that platforms publish per language and per detection category. The Framework is modelled on PCI-DSS for payment security and EPEAT for sustainable electronics — voluntary technical standards that became de facto requirements through procurement reference and regulatory adoption.

The Foundation's independent certification programme will begin in 2026 with pilot participants. The methodology is documented in full in the white paper, available on Zenodo.

Who built this

The founder.

Tanya Becheva is the founder of Foreningen Custorian and the principal author of the Custorian Controls Framework. She is a contributor to the IEEE P3462 working group on child and youth online safety and a member of the IEEE P2089.4 age-appropriate design working group. She holds CISSP candidate status and works in data protection and privacy at a global technology company. She is based in Odense, Denmark, and is a parent of two.

The Foundation is forming its Advisory Council to broaden governance beyond the founder. Council seats are reserved for academic researchers, civil-society organisations, hotline operators, independent child-safety experts, young people with lived experience, and an independent chair. The Council is expected to be operational in 2026.

The model

The Foundation and the implementation market.

Foreningen Custorian develops the Controls Framework, conducts independent assessment, accredits qualified assessors, and operates the public registry. The Foundation is the standards body. It does not sell tools or services to the platforms it assesses.

Implementation of the framework is open. Platforms can build their own compliance using the published standard. Independent vendors can build certified implementations and sell them to platforms. Accredited assessors can conduct assessments commercially. The Foundation maintains structural independence from any single implementer.

The Foundation is structurally separated from the implementation market. This separation is the source of the standard's credibility, and it is enforced by governance, by law, and by funding rules.

Current state

Where things are now.

The Controls Framework v0.1 is published. The white paper is available on Zenodo with a DOI. The methodology is documented in technical detail. The Foundation is incorporated in Denmark. The Advisory Council is forming. The independent assessment programme will begin in 2026 with pilot participants.
