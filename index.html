01

Why we exist

Europe now requires platforms to protect children. It does not tell them how to prove they have.

The Digital Services Act, the AI Act, the AVMSD and a growing set of national codes place real obligations on services used by children. They are written as outcomes: a provider must put in place appropriate and proportionate measures. What none of them supplies is the testable detail underneath, the thing an auditor can hold a product against and reach the same conclusion twice.

That gap is not a drafting oversight, it is how framework legislation works. But it leaves a vacuum, and the vacuum gets filled by self-assessment, by marketing, and by trust marks a company can buy. A parent, a regulator and a procurement officer are all left with the same problem: no way to tell a platform that is safe from one that says it is.

A claim about children's safety should be evidenced, not asserted. Everything Custorian does follows from that one sentence.

The burden of proof is moving onto providers. Custorian exists so that when it lands, there is something rigorous, public and independent for it to land on.

02

What we do

We write and steward the Child Digital Safety and Wellbeing Standard, and we do not certify anyone against it.

CDSW is a technology-neutral control set. Each control is a single obligation with one testable criterion, a required evidence class, and a named legal or technical authority behind it. It is published openly under CC BY-ND 4.0, so the requirements that decide a certificate are public rather than sold.

The control counts, the ten domains, the eleven platform profiles, the authorities each control cites and the three levels of conformity are all set out on how it works.

Alongside the standard

We maintain a risk catalogue of 90 catalogued harms mapped to controls, an authority register recording the duty-bearer behind every citation, and jurisdictional overlays that keep national law out of the core requirements so the standard travels. We publish a register of certified organisations, and we run research on where children are actually harmed rather than where it is convenient to look.

03

How it works

The association that writes the rules is structurally barred from deciding who passes them.

  1. The standard is published. Every control, its test type and its authority are open. Nothing that decides a certificate is behind a paywall.
  2. A platform is assessed against it. The assessment is carried out by an independent certification body accredited to ISO/IEC 17065 by a national accreditation body, not by Custorian.
  3. The certification body decides. Custorian maintains the scheme and licenses the mark to qualifying bodies. It never audits or certifies a platform itself.
  4. The result is public. Certificates, their scope and their withdrawals appear in a public register, because a claim nobody can check is not accountability.
Why the separation matters. If the body that writes a standard also sells the certificate, every commercial pressure runs one way. Separating the two is the difference between a standard and a logo.

04

Vision, mission and values

Our vision
A world in which a platform’s claim about children’s safety can be checked by anyone, rather than taken on trust.
Our mission
To publish and maintain an open standard for children’s digital safety that is specific enough to audit, and to keep it structurally independent of the platforms it measures, so that a certificate means something.

Our strategic objectives

  1. Turn obligations into requirements. Convert the outcome-based duties in the DSA, the AI Act and national codes into control statements specific enough that two auditors reach the same conclusion.
  2. Keep the decision independent. Build and maintain a certification scheme that only externally accredited bodies can operate, so the people who write the rules never decide who passes them.
  3. Put children in the room. Involve children and young people in shaping what the standard requires and how it is communicated, as participants rather than subjects.
  4. Publish the evidence. Release the controls, the risk catalogue, the legal authorities and the consultation dispositions as open data, so the standard can be checked, cited and challenged.

06

Why it cannot be bought

Independence is a structure, not a promise. Four of the mechanisms are load-bearing. The values these follow from are set out above.

  1. We take no money from anyone we could assess. No funding, in any form, from a platform that is or may become subject to assessment, or from anyone controlling or substantially funding one.
  2. We cannot certify. Only external accredited bodies issue certificates. The association is structurally unable to certify itself or anyone else.
  3. Membership buys nothing. No fee tier confers a board seat, a vote on board composition, or certification eligibility. Platforms subject to assessment cannot be members at all.
  4. There are no shares to sell. The association has no shareholders and distributes no profit. The standard stays with it, and remains public even if the association dissolves.

The full set of firewalls, the articles behind them, the decisions the board has taken and the accounts are all published on the governance page.

The registrar scorecard

Nobody publishes a registrar-level record of how the domain industry answers child sexual abuse material. We record the one thing checkable from outside: whether a registrar’s own agreement names it. Not published while it is in review.

In development

On-device detection

Detection for the harm classes that surface in a child’s own messages, running on the child’s device rather than on a server. Trained on public corpora and synthetic conversation, never on real abuse material, and nothing leaves the device. Grooming and self-harm are the first two built. None is deployed until its calibration survives clinical review.

In development

How a child is told

A detection is only useful if the message that follows it is one a young person will accept. We test the wording with a youth panel. The question is never whether to report a child.

Stress-test the standard

A standard nobody has attacked is not a standard, it is a draft with confidence. CDSW is stress-tested by outside expert peer reviewers.

Public review is now closed. Sign-up is by invitation key.

Reviewers are independent specialists who have no stake in the outcome: child-rights practitioners, trust and safety leads, regulators, academics, clinicians, law enforcement, engineers and young people’s advocates. You choose the domains you are qualified to review and the depth you can give, from a targeted read of one section to a deep dive across the control set.

  1. Pick your domains. Ten control domains and eleven platform profiles. You review only what you actually know.
  2. Comment on the record. Comments are submitted clause by clause in the ISO and CEN convention: general, technical or editorial, with the change you propose and the reason for it.
  3. Every comment gets an answer. Each one is dispositioned with a stated reason, accepted or not. Nothing is quietly dropped.
What we ask, and what we do not. Review is unpaid and independent. It is not an endorsement, it does not make you an advisor, and it confers no certification advantage on any organisation you work for. Conflicts are declared at sign-up and recorded. You are credited by name and interest category only if you consent.

Get involved in governance.

The Governance Board is Custorian’s primary governing body, holding ultimate fiduciary and safeguarding accountability across the association, without directing day-to-day operations. Seats are filled by resolution of the sitting Board and presented to the general assembly for ratification (Articles § 3.4 and § 5.1), for renewable two-year terms, unpaid, with four to six Board meetings a year, held virtually. At least two directors must be independent. Directors are expected to bring a strong professional network and help open doors to funding and partnerships. You cannot be employed by, or hold a financial interest in, a platform subject to the standard, or have any employment, contractual, or financial relationship with any Custorian arm, its commercial technology company, or a recognised Certification Body within the last 36 months.

The Advisory Board is a roster of ten to twelve named experts who provide ad hoc, per-task input on the standard, its ethical dimensions, and child-facing content, drawn from child rights, legal, technology & security, academic, clinical, law enforcement, and youth backgrounds. There is no fixed meeting cadence: work happens as discrete review tasks, formalised through an individual MOU. It advises. It does not govern.

Become a member.

Support Membership is the association’s single membership class. The fee is set by who is applying, not by what it buys: the rights are identical at every level. Applying registers your request, it is not a payment. Applications are reviewed quarterly by the Board; if accepted you receive a payment link by email.

What this is asking. Whether you, or the organisation you are joining for, run a consumer digital product that children can use, or could use. That means a platform, app, game, social service, marketplace or AI product that would one day be a candidate for assessment against the standard. It also covers any company that controls one, or is controlled by one.

Why it matters. Custorian writes the standard those products are measured against. If the people being measured could also be members of the association that writes the rules, the standard would be worth less. Articles § 3.1 therefore makes them ineligible for membership outright.

If that is you, answer Yes. You cannot join, but you can still apply to the pilot, ask about reviewer access, or write to us. If you are a business that does not run such a product, for example a law firm, consultancy, foundation or trade body, answer No.

You are not eligible for membership. A platform or service that is, or may become, subject to assessment under the standard cannot be a member of the association, and neither can any company that controls it or is controlled by it (Articles § 3.1). This is not about your intentions. It is the firewall that makes the certificate mean something.

What you can do instead: apply to the pilot, review the standard, which is now by invitation key, or write to info@custorian.org.
