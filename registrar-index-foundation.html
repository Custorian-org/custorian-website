Research · Registrar accountability index

Who answers for a domain?

Every website that has ever hosted child sexual abuse material had a domain name, and every domain name was sold by a registrar. No public index scores those registrars on how seriously they treat it. Abuse frameworks exist, but they are internal, aggregate-only or retired, and none names and scores individual companies in the open. This closes that gap, in four layers, using records that are already public.

How the scoring works

Two rules shape everything below. An absent document is never a bad score, and a voluntary act is never averaged with an imposed finding.

Layer 2: policy specificity

Read from the registrar’s own registration agreement, terms of service or acceptable-use policy, whichever is binding on the registrant. Three outcomes:

  • Names CSAM the document names child sexual abuse material as its own prohibited category.
  • Child-general it names child harm, but not this category specifically.
  • Catch-all it rests on a general clause such as “illegal content”, which also covers phishing and spam.

Every score links the document it was read from and quotes the sentence.

Layer 3a: what they publish

Five components, scored out of 100. Each is something a registrar chose to make public.

25  abuse address, deliverable
25  reporting route named
20  commitment to act or report
15  abuse categories named
15  DSA or trusted-flagger route

A published address whose mail server does not answer takes partial credit. Naming the category without saying what happens next takes half.

Layer 3b: the ICANN record

ICANN publishes every contractual compliance notice it sends, by company name. Those are shown beside the disclosure score and never inside it: one is a voluntary act, the other is imposed, and averaging them would say they are the same kind of fact.

Older notices, from 2013 to 2017, identify a registrar only by name. Where the attachment is our inference rather than ICANN’s identification it is marked inferred, and every notice links to the ICANN letter.

Unscored is not zero

Where no binding document could be read, the registrar is not scored and the reason is shown instead: the site was unreachable, the document was not machine-readable, or no binding document could be identified.

A website with nothing serving on it at all is recorded as a dead ICANN listing, which is a finding about the accreditation register rather than about the company.

This never claims that a named company has hosted abuse material. That is not sourceable at registrar granularity and is not asserted anywhere on this page. Hotlines notify registrars only in rare cases, Project Arachnid names no registrar in its own diagram of the layer, and ICANN’s DNS Abuse definition excludes child sexual abuse material outright.

A registrar whose agreement is silent may still act within minutes of a report. A registrar that names it may be slow. Which of those is true is Layer 4, and nobody publishes it.

Right of reply. Any registrar can contest its entry at info@custorian.org, acknowledged within five working days. Corrections are published with the correction visible.
